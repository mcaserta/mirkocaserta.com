This album exists because of a waltz.

On [Songs](@/music/songs.md) there is "Vals Para Amparo", a tune I dedicated to
my partner Amparo. Back then I wrote that it was a waltz in a minor key, which
doesn't really suit her: Amparo is warm, soulful, solar. The song is none of
those things. I promised myself I'd fix it with something more Latin. A salsa, a
mambo, maybe.

Then she told me that, of everything I've recorded, the one she enjoys the most
is
[Punk Jazz Overdrive](https://mirkocaserta.bandcamp.com/track/punk-jazz-overdrive),
which sits somewhere in afrobeat territory. That settled it. Forget the mambo.

## The tribe

I'm not a drummer, and I'm certainly not a West African percussion ensemble. So
I bought "One Man Tribe Vol. 1", a sample pack of African drums and percussion
by [Ben Aylon](https://benaylon.com/), and built every track on top of his
grooves. Ben plays the entire percussion section by himself, hence the name.

I play everything else: all the instruments, all the parts, nobody else in the
room. One man playing a whole band on top of one man playing a whole drum
circle. The name described what I was doing so well that I stole it for the
album title. Thanks, Ben.

## The tracks

The titles are in Swahili, plus one in Kikongo and Kimbundu. Each grooves on a
different flavour of African or Afro-diasporic rhythm, from straight afrobeat to
Afro-Cuban and Afro-Brazilian.

1. **Jua** (sun). 120 BPM, an Africa vibe.
2. **Moto** (fire). 100 BPM on heavy triplets.
3. **Usiku** (night). 130 BPM, straight afrobeat.
4. **Kalunga**. In Kikongo and Kimbundu, the sea as the threshold between the
   living and the ancestors. 85 BPM, Afro-Brazilian, and the quietest track on
   the record.
5. **Ngoma** (drum, and the music and dance that go with it). 150 BPM,
   Afro-Cuban, the fastest one here.
6. **Pamoja** (together). 100 BPM, an Afro samba.
7. **Moyo** (heart). 130 BPM, an Afro bolero.
8. **Peke Yangu** (by myself). 85 BPM, blues on triplets. A fitting way to close
   an album made alone in a room.

Eight tracks, 27 minutes and change. All instrumental, so the Swahili titles are
the only thing you'll have to pronounce.

## Vol. I

It's called Vol. I because there might be a Vol. II. No promises.
